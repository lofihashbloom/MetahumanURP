# Changelog

## Unreleased

- Package renamed from `com.unity.metahuman` to `metahuman-urp`: this is not a Unity package. Projects that installed it under the old name must remove that entry from `Packages/manifest.json` and add it again from the git URL. Shader includes now use `Packages/metahuman-urp/...`.

## 0.1.0

First release with the sample project: `SampleProject` (Taro talking shot, Git LFS) is published next to the package.

- Package manifest: `repository`, `documentationUrl`, `changelogUrl` and `licensesUrl` point at `github.com/lofihashbloom/MetahumanURP`. The README gives the git URL for installing from that repository (`?path=/MetahumanURP`); that install path has not been tested yet.
- `SkinnedNormalRecompute`: opt-in stretch flattening (`stretchStart`, `stretchFull`; off by default). Where a triangle's skinned area exceeds its rest area by the given ratios, the authored normal-map detail is faded toward the recomputed geometric normal. On Taro's closed eyelids the stretched crease normals read as a dark line; with 1.05 / 1.4 the line is gone. The compute shader and the CPU reference both take the rest area per vertex; `SkinnedNormalsFeature` passes it through.
- `GroomSurfaceBinding`: the per-frame update is a Burst job over the strand vertices, and bindings of the same face share one face `BakeMesh` per frame in Play mode. The package now depends on `com.unity.burst`, `com.unity.collections` and `com.unity.mathematics`. No timing was recorded for this change.
- `GroomDeferredFeature`: with `exclusiveLayers` set, the shader-side cull flag is no longer raised, so grooms of the same shaders on other layers (strands that deform with the skin, which the feature cannot draw) stay on the forward path instead of being hidden.
- Hair self-shadow re-march made cheaper for moving heads (`GroomShadowVolume`). Two changes, both on by default:
  - **Light tolerance** (`lightTolerance`, 0.1 degrees). A light is re-marched once its direction, seen from the nearest point of the volume's bounding sphere, has turned by more than the tolerance since the pose it was marched for; before, any motion re-marched it. 0 restores that. A light inside the bounding sphere always re-marches.
  - **Coarse empty space** (`coarseEmptySpace`, `fullMarchMargin` 2 voxels). Voxels within the margin of a voxel that holds fibres keep the full march (22 to 40% of the volume on the LookDev cast). The others, which strands sample only when displaced out of the groom (sway), march a 4x coarser copy of the density in 4-voxel steps. Off restores the full march everywhere.
  - **Measured** (LookDev, 7 characters with a volume x Rembrandt and stage rig, deferred hair, 1920x1080, Apple M5, editor batchmode, old and new interleaved over 7 rounds, median frame). The re-march was smaller than the 2.5 ms recorded for the forward hair path: with the head turning 1 degree every frame it added 0.2 to 1.5 ms (median 0.5). Head turning 1 degree per frame (tolerance never applies): 0.0 to 0.7 ms faster, median 0.2, 12 of 14 cells; under the stage rig 0.2 to 0.7 ms on six characters, Celeste within noise. Head drifting 0.06 degrees per frame (the tolerance skips about every other march): 0.1 to 1.1 ms faster, median 0.6, 14 of 14 cells. Static head: unchanged.
  - **Image.** Static and turning head: same as before within the run-to-run noise of the stochastic hair (0 to 29 of 2.07M pixels, as between two identical captures; Celeste, which is deterministic, 0 to 1 pixel). Drifting head, transmittance up to 0.1 degrees stale: 85 to 2843 pixels differ, 3 to 33 of them by more than 2/255. Taro talking shot (v16 look, frames 170 to 183) with the volume switched in for the test: old and new march identical (largest difference 0.1 display level). The shot's default path does not use the volume and matched the v16 frames (largest difference 0.2 level).
- Skin lighting in one light loop (`MHSkinLighting`, `MetaHumanSkinLighting.hlsl`). Each light is fetched once per skin pixel, so its shadow map and hair receiver field are sampled once, and the lit colour, the diffuse part for the skin diffusion, and the scatter, fuzz and teeth-translucency terms all come from that loop. Before, the two-lobe path of the single skin draw ran URP's full PBR three times (primary lobe, the same without albedo to extract the diffuse, secondary lobe) and then looped over the lights a fourth time for scatter and fuzz; the desktop path computed one PBR result it then discarded. The reflection probe is now sampled twice (once per lobe) instead of three times. The diffuse-only and forward passes use the same loop. `MHReceiverSurfacePBR` and the `MH_SURFACE_PBR` macro are gone (eyes and hair cards call `UniversalFragmentPBR` directly), and eyes no longer run the card-highlight light loop, which added nothing for them.
  - **LookDev cast** (8 characters, 1920x1080, MSAA 1, Apple M5, editor batchmode, old and new shader variants interleaved over 7 rounds, median frame). With hair hidden, faster in all 20 cells: 1.6 to 2.9 ms under the four-light stage rig with receiver shadows (for example Celeste 9.68 to 6.91 ms, Kellan 8.10 to 6.16 ms), 0.2 to 1.1 ms under Rembrandt (one shadowed light and a fill), and 0.25 to 0.9 ms under Clean and Red/Blue (Celeste and Kellan only). Whole frame with hair: 1.3 to 3.5 ms under the stage rig for every character; under the other setups the gain is within run-to-run noise on some characters (three of 20 cells measured slower on the median, each faster on its best frame).
  - **Image.** Against the previous loops, 0 to 7 of 2.07M pixels differ per frame, by 1/255 (hair hidden; 9 cells checked, both skin paths). In float, a lit fixture differs by at most one half-float step at the specular peak (mean 3e-6) across both skin paths, teeth translucency, the receiver keyword and the single-draw, two-draw and forward-only modes; that check ran under Forward+ only.
- `GroomSurfaceBinding.Bind` finds each strand vertex's closest face triangle through a uniform grid instead of scanning every nearby triangle. Rings of cells are searched outwards until no unsearched triangle can be closer, and ties go to the lowest triangle index as before, so the attachments are unchanged (new test against a brute-force scan). Taro's facial grooms (602k strand vertices; the goatee alone 373k, with a 12 cm search radius): a teaser render that had not finished binding after 10 minutes now completes in 52 s, startup included.
- Prototype deferred groom renderer `GroomDeferredFeature`. Strands are rasterized without lighting, and each hair pixel is shaded once by its material's `StrandRadiance`, then composited with the all-strand optical depth.
  - **Compute mode (default).** One thread per strand segment rasterizes the ribbon quad at pixel centres, with the same placement as the compensated-rectangle vertex stage. It scans along the segment's major screen axis, so a segment costs its length, not its bounding box. Optical depth uses an atomic add and the nearest strand an atomic max of depth, followed by an id pass. Points and segments are built once on the GPU from the ribbon meshes.
  - **Long segments.** Ribbon meshes whose mean segment length projects to `longSegmentPixels` or more (default 12) are drawn by the GPU's rasterizer instead, with their own index buffer (`Hidden/MetaHuman/GroomLongSegments`). These are long strands with few points per strand. The shade pass takes the nearer of the two results. If that shader is missing or unsupported (for example stripped from a player build), every mesh stays on compute.
  - **Per-renderer data.** Raster parameters come from each renderer's material with its property-block overrides, such as a per-renderer `_StrandFraction`. Shading draws one screen rectangle per shade group: the renderers sharing a material and a `GroomShadowVolume`, shaded with the group's property block.
  - **Hardware mode:** an opaque attribute pass plus the optical-depth pass.
  - **LookDev cast** (8 characters x Rembrandt and stage rig, 1920x1080, MSAA 1, Apple M5, editor), whole frame: 2.3 to 10.9 ms cheaper in every cell. For example, Celeste goes from 22.1 to 18.6 ms, Trey from 17.9 to 13.3 ms and Ada from 23.6 to 12.8 ms (Rembrandt). The image matches the nearest-layer path: mean 0.07–0.25/255 over hair, and at most 90 pixels per frame differ by more than 32/255.
  - **Ada v18 stage** (2.58M segments): whole frame 18.8 to 10.6–10.8 ms. Unreal 5.8 with matched features measured 16.2 ms just before.
  - **Forward passes.** The forward-path groom passes cull themselves in the vertex stage while the feature draws the camera, so grooms left on `GroomTransparencyFeature`'s layers still render once. That culling still runs every strand's vertex stage; see `exclusiveLayers` below.
  - **Per-frame work.** Only the compute-rasterized meshes' segments are dispatched, and the id pass skips segments that never raised the nearest depth in the first pass. `settings.exclusiveLayers` lets the feature claim its layers: `GroomTransparencyFeature` skips them for the cameras the feature draws, instead of running every strand's vertex stage to cull it. On the LookDev cast (8 characters x 2 setups, idle machine, median over cells), the previous behaviour was slower by 1.1 ms for the forward-path culling (in every cell), 0.35 ms for the full id pass (up to 1.2 ms on Ada), and 0.16 ms for the full dispatch (within noise). Images are unchanged except for pixels where two strands tie for nearest (at most 21 per frame).
  - **Opt-in.** Shaders opt in through `GroomDeferredPasses.hlsl`; the LookDev `AdaHairShadow-v27` and `TeaserHair-v34` shaders do so today. `LookDevSceneBuilder` adds the feature to the cast renderer and puts the deferred grooms on their own layer (29), claimed with `exclusiveLayers` and removed from the renderer's opaque layer mask.
  - **Limits:** single-sample cameras; compute mode needs every groom material on the rectangle profile; rigid grooms; Metal only. See the HairDeferred notes (not part of this repository).
- Single-draw skin diffusion (`SkinDiffusionFeature.settings.singleDraw`, on by default). Skin is drawn once, right after the opaques, by a new `MetaHumanSkinSplit` pass that writes the lit colour and the three diffusion inputs (diffuse + amount, normal + depth, albedo) together. Its forward pass culls itself in the vertex stage for those cameras (global `_MHSkinSingleDraw`, reset per camera). Before, skin was drawn twice: the forward pass, then a diffuse-only extraction draw. The legacy two-lobe path now does three PBR evaluations in one draw, against four over two draws. Desktop skin lighting and the scatter/fuzz extra lights each take one light loop for both outputs. The filters and the composite are unchanged. The single draw needs `layers` = Everything; otherwise the two-draw path is used. A feature that requests the depth texture at exactly `AfterRenderingOpaques` without a depth prepass would get its copy before the skin. Ada v18 stage, groom off (1920x1080, Apple M5, Editor, Metal, 15 interleaved rounds): skin diffusion cost MSAA1 3.31 to 1.59 ms, MSAA4 4.31 to 2.10 ms. Images against the two-draw path: MSAA1 1,079 pixels differ, at most 3/255; MSAA4 about 2,500, of which one exceeds 8/255 (9/255). `settings.additiveComposite` (on by default) blends the diffusion change onto the camera colour instead of compositing into a copy. MSAA1 is bit-identical, MSAA4 keeps per-sample edges (104 pixels differ), and the time difference is within noise (+-0.1 ms). New GPU tests: `SkinSingleDrawTests`.
- Hair self-shadowing for `GroomStrands`: new `GroomShadowVolume` component and `GroomShadowFeature` renderer feature. The volume deposits each strand segment's mean projected fibre area (pi/4 x diameter x length) into a half-float extinction grid in a head-local frame (96 voxels on the longest axis by default), once, on the CPU. The feature's compute pass (`MetaHumanGroomShadowTransmittance`) precomputes, for up to four lights per volume, the optical depth from every voxel toward the light, and re-marches a light only when it moves relative to the volume. Strands multiply each direct light by exp(-tau) from one trilinear fetch per light. Lights are matched by identity through the camera's additional-light buffer; per-volume bindings live in per-material-index property blocks, so several characters render together. A camera whose renderer lacks the feature renders strands unshadowed. Scratch URP project, procedural 20k-strand groom (460k segments), Apple M5 Editor, Metal: density build 0.22 s; both lights' march (70x96x63) about 1 ms wall including readback sync; under a directional key, changed hair pixels are 14% darker on average and none brighter. Automatic light selection includes tool-created `HideFlags.DontSave` lights. The density survives disable (switching a character off and on does not rebuild it) and is freed on destroy. LookDev (7 MetaHuman characters x Rembrandt and red/blue presets, 1920x1080, Apple M5 Editor): same look as the teaser shader's 24-step per-pixel density march (Celeste red/blue: 119 of 1M pixels differ by more than 8/255) and a median 2.0 ms per frame cheaper (cheaper in 11 of 14 cells; Celeste red/blue 40.0 to 33.3 ms); re-marching every light every frame adds a median 2.5 ms; density build 0.35-1.7 s per character, once. Gray occluders only: no fibre colour, no multiple scattering, rigid strands. Skin still uses the static receiver fields. See the HairSelfShadow notes (not part of this repository).
- Strand LOD. New `_StrandFraction` material property on `GroomStrands`: it draws that fraction of the strands, chosen by each strand's stable random value, and widens the kept ones by 1/fraction to keep coverage. Dropped strands exit at the top of the vertex shader. New `GroomStrandLod` component: sets the fraction from the groom's on-screen height (full detail at `fullDetailScreenHeight`, default 35%; fraction = (height / full-detail height)^exponent in 1/8 steps; `minimumFraction` default 1/4; hysteresis), on per-component material copies of the listed renderers. Ada v18 stage (5.2M strand triangles, 1920x1080, Apple M5, Editor), groom cost: 1/2 of the strands 17.0 to 11.0 ms at MSAA1 and 37.0 to 25.4 ms at MSAA4; 1/4 of the strands 8.6 and 17.8 ms. Look: at 2.5x the portrait distance (groom at 14% of the screen height) 1/2 is hard to tell from full detail, but the hair is about 10% darker (fewer specular glints); in the 35% close-up, 1/2 is visibly coarser. Keep close-ups at full detail and list only the scalp groom (thinned eyebrows read heavy).
- Skin diffuse extraction (`MetaHumanSkinDiffuse`, legacy two-lobe path with `_DesktopSkinLighting` 0): two light-loop evaluations instead of four. The diffuse term (albedo x (1 - reflectivity)) does not depend on smoothness, so the two-lobe mix of (with albedo - without albedo) equals the single-lobe difference. Ada v18 stage, 1920x1080, Apple M5, Editor, groom off: skin diffusion cost MSAA1 3.52 to 2.49 ms, MSAA4 4.79 to 3.34 ms. Images are identical: 2 pixels differ by 1/255 at MSAA1, none at MSAA4. Desktop skin lighting already evaluated diffuse once.
- Measured and left as is: the skin diffusion blur and composite passes skip non-skin pixels and cost little (a precomputed-tap radial filter and in-place composite measured no gain); the eye scene-color snapshot costs about 0.1 ms.
- `GroomStrands`: continuous-coverage materials (`_CoverageMode` 1) no longer rasterize in URP's forward and depth-only passes. Those passes clipped every fragment, because continuous coverage is drawn only by `GroomTransparencyFeature`, but every strand was still rasterized. Their vertex stage (`StrandCulledVertex`) now exits early with an off-screen position, so the triangles are culled before rasterization. No material setup is needed; stochastic-fallback materials are unchanged. Ada v18 stage with the v27 hair (5.3M strand triangles, 1920x1080, Apple M5, Editor, Metal), whole frame: MSAA1 about 32 to 22.8 ms, MSAA4 about 55 to 41.5 ms, MSAA8 about 58 to 53.5 ms. MSAA1 is bit-identical. At MSAA4/8 the hair matches, but 15k pixels on opaque silhouette edges change by a mean of -0.02/255 (34 pixels exceed 8/255); the cause is not yet isolated. Custom strand shaders built from this one should use the same vertex entry for their forward and depth-only passes.
- Tried and not adopted: strand-LOD prefix in the deferred groom. Each mesh's segments were sorted by their strand's keep threshold at build, so a strand fraction dispatched and drew only the kept segments instead of letting dropped strands exit in the shader. LookDev cast at the 0.625 scalp budget (26 to 37% of segments dropped; 1920x1080, MSAA 1, Apple M5, 7 interleaved rounds): slower on the characters drawn mostly by the GPU rasterizer (Celeste +1.5 ms, Sunita +0.5 to 0.8 ms, Jelena +0.3 ms, with a stable sort that keeps each strand's segments in order; about twice that with an unstable one) and no change on an all-compute character (Kellan, within 0.1 ms). Dropped strands are close to free in the main view, and reordering strands by threshold costs the rasterizer more than skipping them saves. The shadow pass, where dropped strands were measured at 1.7 to 2.2 ms, is a separate case: a shadow-only mesh holding the kept strands in their original order would not reorder anything.
- Tried and not adopted: spatially compact strand chunks (strands regrouped by Morton order of their centroid) with per-camera culling of chunks hidden behind a conservative skull ellipsoid. Images were bit-identical, but on the Ada stage the occluder hid only 26-50 of 532 chunks (5-10%), and the extra draws of smaller chunks cancelled the saving: -1.3 to +1 ms against the original 89 chunks across front, three-quarter and side views (within noise).
- Tried and not adopted: rendering the nearest-layer groom targets at half resolution saved 0.5-2 ms at MSAA1 (within noise) and softened the hair. On this stage, groom cost is dominated by per-strand geometry work, not pixel count.
- Groom `NearestLayer` depth-tested selection (`settings.nearestSelection`, default `Auto`). A depth-only prepass (`MetaHumanGroomNearestDepthPrepass`) writes the nearest strand per sample into a copy of the camera depth. An optical-depth-only pass (`MetaHumanGroomOpticalDepth`) follows. The radiance pass (`MetaHumanGroomNearestRadianceDepthTested`) then depth-tests against the stored depth, so only the nearest layer is shaded and MSAA coverage comes from the per-sample depth test. This replaces the nearest-depth texture loads and the `SV_Coverage` output. `Auto` uses it on multisampled targets only: at MSAA1 the extra geometry pass makes it slower. Hard selection only: material radiance depth bands are ignored on this path. Temporal reconstruction keeps the shader path. Measured on the Ada v18 stage with the v27 hair shader (1920x1080, Apple M5, Editor, Metal), whole frame: MSAA4 64.6 to 46.8 ms, MSAA8 86.8 to 57.8 ms, MSAA1 unchanged under `Auto` (forcing `DepthTest` costs +3.5 ms). Image difference against the shader path: under 1% of hair pixels differ by more than 8/255 (isolated samples where strands tie in depth). Custom groom shaders need the three new passes, or should set `Shader`.

## 0.1.0-exp.17

- Add hair-to-skin receiver shadows: `HairReceiverShadowFeature`, `HairReceiverShadowVolume`, `HairReceiverShadowField` and the local `MH_HAIR_RECEIVER_SHADOW` skin keyword. Static light-space optical depth of projected fiber area attenuates each mapped direct light once, in both forward and skin-diffuse passes. Lights map to slots by identity through the actual additional-light buffer. Receiver variants evaluate every skin PBR lobe through `MHReceiverSurfacePBR` (URP's light loop via public functions). Off by default; variants without the keyword are unchanged.
- Add `Tools~/Hair/build_receiver_shadow_fields.py` and an Editor importer for its `Fields.json`. The builder reproduces the validated host fields byte for byte.
- Groom `NearestLayer` performance: on MSAA targets, the nearest-depth and nearest-radiance passes now run once per pixel. They select samples by pixel-center depth through `SV_Coverage` instead of shading every sample with `SV_SampleIndex`. In nearest-layer mode, accumulation writes only optical depth (its radiance was discarded). Ada at 1200 px MSAA8: 2256 ms to 111 ms per frame (20x), mean luminance equal to 1e-6 and 0.3% L1 difference (isolated samples). Metal cannot translate `GetRenderTargetSamplePosition`/`Count`, so neither is used.
- Groom `NearestLayer` single geometry pass (`singlePassNearestDepth`, on by default): a new `MetaHumanGroomOpticalDepthNearest` shader pass writes all-strand optical depth (additive) and nearest-layer depth (`BlendOp 1 Min`) together, without lighting, replacing the separate accumulation and nearest-depth passes. The result is bit-identical at MSAA1. Ada 1200 px: MSAA1 38.6 to 33.2 ms, MSAA8 101.9 to 96.1 ms. Custom groom shaders need this pass, or should turn the setting off.
- Rig-driven wrinkles (animated maps): new local skin keyword `MH_ANIMATED_MAPS` blends three wrinkle normal maps and three colour maps per region, as default + sum_N (map_N - default) * mask_N. Each mask is the sum over slices of the rig weights dotted with a `_WrinkleMaskArray` slice (Texture2DArray, 4 regions per slice), following the MetaHuman head material's documented scheme. New `DnaAnimatedMapBinding` copies `NativeRig.AnimatedMaps` into the per-renderer `_AnimatedMapWeights` and matches DNA names (`head_wm1_normal.head_wm1_blink_L`) or the material form with an underscore. Wrinkle normals now use the main normal map's UV and green-sign conventions. Variants without the keyword are unchanged. Ada: all 41 regions bind; forehead and eye creases appear on brow raise and squint as in Unreal.
- Corrective blend shapes: `DnaRigDriver` applies the rig's blend-shape outputs (`applyBlendShapes`, on by default) to the face mesh's matching shapes. Mesh shapes named `<mesh>_lodN_mesh__<channel>` map to rig channels by name, one channel per mesh, with weights x100; rest weights are restored on shutdown. Ada: 821/821 shapes bound, and weights match Unreal's morph curves within about 0.01. The importer now imports blend shapes without normal deltas (`ModelImporterNormals.None`): the FBX normal deltas shaded eyelid folds black, and Unity-calculated deltas left a dark sliver. Joint poses were checked against Unreal on the same frames: median 0.06 mm rigid residual over 234 joints and 0.15 mm motion difference.
- `DnaRigDriver.layerBodyJoints` (on by default): DNA joints outside the face (`neck_01`, `head`, spine, clavicles, arms) now layer the rig's deltas on their current animated local pose instead of the DNA neutral, so body animation keeps driving the neck and head while the face rig runs. A joint keeps its last base when nothing else wrote it, so deltas never accumulate. `FACIAL_` joints are unchanged.
- Add `GroomSurfaceBinding`: a CPU reference that makes a short strand mesh (eyebrows) follow the deforming face skin. Each strand vertex is attached at rest to its closest face triangle, with its offset, strand direction and material frame kept in the triangle frame, and rebuilt from one `BakeMesh` per update. Ada brows: 24,372 vertices, at most 2.4 mm from the skin. It is not Unreal's groom binding (no RBF, no GPU skin cache).
- Validated on Ada at 1200 px (Metal, Forward+, four spots, MSAA8). Fields are rest pose: no head motion, deformation, simulation, moving lights or fiber color. See the HairReceiverShadows notes (not part of this repository).

## 0.1.0-exp.16

- Add opt-in authored-roughness0.37 local response envelope, independently validated on fresh unit materials and both long-hair views. Preserve profile1 and default0. Document remaining brightness, mixed unit-angle errors, nonreciprocity and local-only energy bounds.
- Feed covered groom depth and static/rigid velocity into URP TAA. Add camera history invalidation on cuts, projection/target changes and explicit geometry changes; preserve AA-off/default rendering.

- Keep true spatial reference supersampling separate from temporal reconstruction. Add matched native temporal references and continuous-frame host validation outside the distributable.


## 0.1.0-exp.15

Adds an independently implemented compensated-rectangle raster profile derived from area compensation and verified source behavior. The host adds original shoulder-length procedural groom fixtures and matched Unreal component references; character data remains outside the package.
